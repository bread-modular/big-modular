# Knobs (3D print)

Push-on knob for the user-stock RV09 tall trimmer pots (plain 6 mm knurled shaft), in four widths (Ø16, Ø13, Ø11.5 and Ø10 mm), plus a puller to take them
off again. Generated by `tools/build_knobs.sh`; previews in `renders/knob*.png`
(`knob-widths.png`: the four widths side by side).
**Grip 4 (2026-11-11). Fit to print: `h4`** (user's pick on the Bambu Lab P2S, 2026-11-12; see
"Fit ladder" below). Not every width is test-printed, and the new fit gauges are not. Grips 1 and 2 (slit sleeve with spring fingers) were loose in the
user's prints. Grip 3 (square tapered bore, friction only) held in its tightest fit but turned on
the shaft when the pot was turned hard against its end stop. Grip 4 is keyed to the shaft knurl:
see "Grip" below. Of its first gauge (`fit1` .. `fit8`, bore and teeth shrinking together) the
user's print of the loosest tile, `fit8`, dropped into the knurl and locked ("workable"). The
`t1` .. `t8` knobs that followed kept that tile's round bore and varied only the teeth; the slim
`t4` knob (the old `fit8`) matched the tile in the user's print.

**Fit ladder `h1` .. `h8` (2026-11-12).** The tightest knob of the `t` ladder, `t1`,
was "not too tight" on the user's pots (slimmer `t1`), and the user asked for a new range with
`t1` in the middle and a hole that gets smaller too. `h5` is that `t1`. Toward `h1` the whole hole
closes in, round bore and teeth together; `h6` .. `h8` are the old `t2` .. `t4`. The old `t5` ..
`t8` (looser still) are no longer built.

**`h4` is the fit for the user's P2S** (user print, 2026-11-12: "H4 is the ideal"; round bore
Ø6.15, tooth tips Ø5.40 at the roof). Next time print `<width>/knob-<width>-h4.stl` or
`<width>/two-color/knob-<width>-h4-two-color.3mf` directly, without a gauge. The width of that
print is not recorded here, and the pick is for that printer and its filament and settings: on
another printer, another material or a width that turns out different, go back to the gauge.

| New | `h1` .. `h4` | `h5` | `h6` | `h7` | `h8` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Old | new, tighter (`h4` = the user's pick) | `t1` | `t2` | `t3` | `t4` (= `fit8`) |

## Shape

| mm | `wide` | `slim` | `slimmer` | `slimmest` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base / top diameter | 16.0 / 15.0 | 13.0 / 12.2 | 11.5 / 10.8 | 10.0 / 9.4 |
| Height | 13.5 | 13.5 | 13.5 | 13.5 |
| Flutes (depth) | 20 (0.4) | 16 (0.3) | 14 (0.25) | 12 (0.2) |
| Pointer pocket, off the centre | 0.7 to 6.1 | 0.7 to 4.7 | 0.7 to 4.0 | 0.7 to 3.3 |
| Solid wall between the bore and a flute, minimum | 4.07 | 2.76 | 2.10 | 1.44 |
| Flat base face around the bore mouth | 4.15 | 2.65 | 1.90 | 1.15 |

- 0.6 mm top chamfer. Pointer pocket on the top face: 1.6 mm wide, 0.6 mm deep, closed at both
  ends (0.8 mm rim) so paint-pen ink stays in it. On the slimmest knob it is only 2.6 mm long.
- Every width has the same bores, teeth and height; only the shell differs. The knob is solid: no
  slits, pockets or thin walls. The wall behind the teeth is asserted >= 1.35 mm (three printed
  lines) for the widest bore, Ø6.2; the tighter fits have up to 0.1 mm more.
- **The wall is what holds the teeth in the grooves.** `slimmer` has three quarters of the slim
  knob's wall and `slimmest` half of it, so the same fit number can feel softer on them and a
  forced-on tight fit can split the slimmest one. Not measured. `h4` is the user's pick
  (2026-11-12, width not recorded): start there on every width, and print that width's gauge
  only if `h4` does not fit it the same.
- Bore 11.5 mm deep (2.0 mm roof, 1.4 mm under the pointer pocket).

## Grip

A round bore with teeth at the top that drop into the grooves of the shaft knurl, like the
moulded push-on caps the user pointed to (Thonk Tall Trimmer Toppers). The knob is keyed to the
shaft, so it cannot turn on it (`renders/knob-grip-section.png`,
`knob-spline-section.png`, `knob-slim-underside.png`; `knob-teeth-compare.png` still shows the
old ladder: its `t1` is `h5`, its `t4` is `h8`, its `t8` is no longer built). The grip is the same
in all four widths:

- **The shaft**: Ø6.0 +0 / -0.1 plastic, with a fine straight knurl of 40 teeth on the top 7 mm
  (Song Huei R0904N, shaft type KQ; Thonk sells the tall trimmer as `R0904N-...-KQ`). The 40 teeth
  are from the data sheet, not counted on the user's pots, and the groove depth is not on the
  drawing: 0.2 mm is assumed.
- **20 teeth in the top 6.0 mm of the bore**, one for every second knurl groove. 40 teeth at a
  0.47 mm pitch are finer than a 0.4 mm nozzle can print; 20 at 0.97 mm are not. Each tooth is a
  90 deg triangle, 0.3 to 0.375 mm high at the bore roof (base 0.6 to 0.75 mm, at least 0.19 mm
  of bore wall between two teeth).
- **The tooth tips close in toward the roof**: 0.3 mm on the diameter over the 6 mm (1.4 deg per
  side). The teeth find the grooves loosely and wedge into them as the knob is pushed on, so the
  knob has no play once it is seated. Below the teeth they run out over 0.6 mm.
- **The bore itself is round**: Ø6.2 where the teeth stand in `h5` .. `h8` (the bore of the tile
  that went on in the user's print), 0.05 mm smaller per step from `h4` down to Ø6.0 in `h1`,
  which is the nominal shaft. The mouth is 0.1 mm wider (0.3 mm chamfer). The bore guides the
  plain shaft below the knurl; a smaller bore leaves the knob less room to rock.
- **Solid wall behind the teeth.** For the knob to slip, the wall would have to stretch enough
  for 20 teeth to climb out of their grooves, or the teeth would have to shear off.
- The knob goes on in 9 deg steps (40 grooves), so the pointer can be set within 4.5 deg.
- A fit is one step of 0.05 mm on the diameter (0.025 mm per side). The tooth tips step all the
  way from `h8` to `h1`. From `h8` to `h5` the bore stays at Ø6.2, so the teeth get taller; from
  `h5` to `h1` the bore steps with the tips, so the whole hole gets smaller and the teeth keep
  their height. Deeper tooth tips wedge into the grooves earlier: one step moves the seat 1.0 mm
  along the shaft.

| Fit (gauge notches) | Round bore | Tooth tips, roof / 6 mm below | Tooth height at the roof | On a Ø5.9 shaft | On a Ø6.0 shaft |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `h1` (1) | 6.00 | 5.25 / 5.55 | 0.375 | stops 4.2 mm short of the roof | does not go on |
| `h2` (2) | 6.05 | 5.30 / 5.60 | 0.375 | stops 3.2 mm short | stops 5.1 mm short |
| `h3` (3) | 6.10 | 5.35 / 5.65 | 0.375 | stops 2.2 mm short | stops 4.1 mm short |
| **`h4` (4), the user's pick on the P2S** | 6.15 | 5.40 / 5.70 | 0.375 | seats 1.2 mm short | stops 3.1 mm short |
| **`h5` (5) = old `t1`, the middle** | 6.20 | 5.45 / 5.75 | 0.375 | seats 0.2 mm short | stops 2.1 mm short |
| `h6` (6) = old `t2` | 6.20 | 5.50 / 5.80 | 0.35 | on the roof, light | seats 1.1 mm short |
| `h7` (7) = old `t3` | 6.20 | 5.55 / 5.85 | 0.325 | on the roof, light | seats 0.1 mm short |
| `h8` (8) = old `t4` / `fit8` | 6.20 | 5.60 / 5.90 | 0.30 | loose | on the roof, light (0.02 mm play) |

- The table is geometry only, for a print exactly to size: V grooves 0.2 mm deep, tooth tips
  rounded to half a 0.45 mm line. Play is radial, tooth tip to groove, with the shaft tip on the
  roof. Not measured.
- **Do not pick from the table.** It has two data points and misses one of them. It calls `h8`
  (the old `fit8`) "on the roof, light" on a Ø6.0 shaft, and that tile locked in the user's
  print. It has `h5` (the old `t1`) stopping 2.1 mm short on a Ø6.0 shaft, and the user's slimmer
  `t1` went on and was "not too tight". It has `h4` stopping 3.1 mm short on a Ø6.0 shaft, and
  `h4` is the fit the user picked as ideal. So the prints are looser than the table, or the shaft
  is nearer Ø5.9; the shaft diameter and the groove depth have still not been measured. That is
  why the ladder goes on past the fits the table says are too tight, `h1` included.
- 0.05 mm on the diameter is close to what a printer repeats; neighbouring fits may feel alike.
  The seat height tells them apart: 1 mm per step once the teeth wedge.
- A knob that seats before the shaft tip reaches the roof sits higher by that amount. It holds
  the same; only the gap under it grows.
- **The knob no longer slips, so the pot's end stop takes what the hand gives.** The stop is rated
  for 3 kgf·cm (0.29 N·m, R0904N data sheet); the pot turns at 30 to 150 gf·cm. Grip 3 slipped
  before that; grip 4 will not. The shaft is rated for a 4 to 5 kgf push or pull.

## Fit gauge

The gauge of a width is that width's eight knobs in one print (two rows of four, `h1` .. `h4`
behind, `h5` .. `h8` in front), joined by 1.2 x 0.6 mm break-off links on the bed
(`renders/knob-fit-gauge.png`). A gauge knob **is** the knob of that fit: same shell,
roof, bore, teeth and bed face, printed the same way up. The only difference is its number:
1 .. 8 notches (1 x 1 x 1.2 mm) in the base rim, each in the ridge between two flutes, up to four
side by side and the fifth and up on the opposite side of the rim. The base faces the panel, so a
gauge knob that fits can stay on the pot.

| Gauge | Size on the bed, mm | Plastic |
|---|---|---|
| `wide` | 73 x 35 | 16.6 cm³ |
| `slim` | 61 x 29 | 10.2 cm³ |
| `slimmer` | 55 x 26 | 7.5 cm³ |
| `slimmest` | 49 x 23 | 5.0 cm³ |

**Why the gauge changed (2026-11-11).** The earlier gauges were tiles: the bore in a Ø12 boss on
a 12 x 14 mm plate. The user reported "the sizes on the fit gauge and the knobs are not correct",
read here as: a tile and the knob of the same number did not fit alike. In CAD they were alike: on the exported STEP files, the bore sections of every tile and of the slim knob
of the same number agree at seven heights (tooth tip and bore diameter to 0.001 mm). So the
difference came from the print, not from the model, and its cause is not confirmed. What differed
between tile and knob: the body around the bore, and the bore roof (where the teeth are deepest
and grip) 1.1 mm above the bed in a tile but 2.0 mm in a knob. The new gauge removes both, and it
was checked the same way on the `t` ladder: 9 sections per knob, 4 widths x 8 fits, no difference
(bore and outline; not repeated for the `h` ladder, which builds the gauge knobs with the same
code). What it cannot remove: eight knobs printed together
cool longer per layer than one or four. If a single knob still fits differently from its gauge
knob, print the knobs four at a time or trust the single knob.

## Puller

Spring tongs, 28.6 x 57.8 x 10 mm: two 2.8 mm legs under an arch, a 0.8 mm hook 2 mm long on each
foot, grip ridges on the legs. The opening is 18 mm, so it drops over every knob width.

1. Stand it over the knob with one leg above and one below it (in line with the pots in its column),
   feet on the panel. That is the only way round with room for it: the neighbouring big knobs
   are in the way sideways.
2. Squeeze the ridges: the hooks slide under the knob base from both sides.
3. Keep squeezing and pull straight up. The knob comes off in the tongs.

| Nominal, mm | `wide` | `slim` | `slimmer` | `slimmest` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook tips closed, off the shaft axis | 6.2 | 4.7 | 3.95 | 3.5 |
| Squeeze force (PLA, E 2.5 GPa) | 3.9 N | 6.1 N | 7.1 N | 7.8 N |
| Hook top to knob base, FR4 / printed panel | 0.9 / 0.5 | 0.8 / 0.4 | 0.75 / 0.35 | 0.7 / 0.3 |
| Hooks to the pot shaft | 3.2 | 1.7 | 0.95 | 0.5 |
| Knob base over the two hooks | 17 mm² | 15 mm² | 14 mm² | 9 mm² |
| Hook tip-up when closed | 0 deg | 1.8 deg | 2.7 deg | 3.2 deg |

- The hooks pull at two opposite points, so the knob lifts straight and the shaft is not levered.
  They are loaded at the root, beside the knob, which is why 0.8 mm is enough under it.
- Free tongs on the panel: 2.2 mm to the small knob above (Ø11 assumed) and to the rail screw heads,
  2.5 mm to the standoff screw heads.
- The hooks hang 3.3 deg when free and are level once closed on the Ø16 knob. Legs and arch are
  one spring: 0.4 % to 0.7 % strain closed on the knobs, 1.2 % with the hook tips squeezed
  together (beam model in the script, not measured).
- **On the slimmest knob the hooks have the least to hold**: its base face is 1.15 mm wide, so
  they stop 0.5 mm from the shaft with 9 mm² under the knob (asserted >= 8 mm²). Squeeze until the
  legs touch the knob, not harder. Sections: `renders/knob-puller-section.png` and
  `-slim-`, `-slimmer-`, `-slimmest-section.png`.
- The puller's shape did not change with the measured shaft, with grips 3 and 4, with the tooth
  ladders or with the two new widths (the STL is byte-identical); it was re-checked on the `h8`
  knob (the old `t4`) of every width. The gap under the knob
  (1.7 mm on FR4, 1.3 mm on the 2.0 mm printed panel) follows the measured shaft and grows by up
  to 2 mm if the knob seats before the roof; if the hooks do not go under, `PULL_HOOK_T` in the
  script is the knob to turn.
- A keyed, wedged knob may need a firmer pull than grip 3 did. Not measured.

## Two-colour knobs

Every width and fit also comes as a two-colour knob in `<width>/two-color/`, for a printer with
a second filament; sized for the Bambu Lab P2S with the 0.4 mm nozzle and an AMS. The pointer is
printed in its own colour instead of being a pocket filled with paint pen.

- **Pointer**: a bar 1.8 mm wide with round ends, from the knob centre out to 1.0 mm short of the
  edge of the top face, 1.0 mm deep (5.9 / 4.5 / 3.8 / 3.1 mm long, 9.9 / 7.4 / 6.1 / 4.9 mm² on
  the bed for wide / slim / slimmer / slimmest). It does not reach the chamfer or the fluted
  side: the body closes around it in every layer, with room for two first-layer lines between
  the pointer and the edge. The body has no pocket: body and pointer together are the plain knob
  without the pocket (asserted: no overlap, no gap), so the bore, the teeth and the fits are
  those of the plain knob of the same number, and the gauge is unchanged.
- **Five layers**: the top face is on the bed and 1.0 mm is the first five layers at 0.20 mm
  layer height (first layer 0.2 mm in every P2S 0.4 mm nozzle process profile), so the pointer
  filament is in layers 1 to 5 and nowhere else. The roof under the pointer is 1.0 mm.
- **History** (user prints, 2026-11-12): the first build (1.6 x 0.6 mm inlay as a separate 3MF
  object) was too small for the slicer; the second (2.4 mm wide out to the edge, first layer
  only, slimmer `t1` printed) had a pointer that was too wide and fitted the shaft like the plain
  slimmer `t1`; the third (1.8 mm wide out to the edge, three layers) was not printed before the
  user asked for five layers and a pointer that stays away from the edge.
- **3MF**: `knob-<width>-hN-two-color.3mf` is one object with two parts, `body` (filament 1) and
  `pointer` (filament 2), already in print orientation. As two separate objects OrcaSlicer loaded
  the pointer as a tiny object of its own (its log: `Object_2 is too close to others`).
- **Checked with OrcaSlicer 2.4.2 from the command line** (import and slicing, not the GUI), P2S
  0.4 nozzle, two PLA filaments: all 32 files load as one object with two parts and filaments 1 /
  2. Sliced `h1`, `h5`, `h8` of each width with `0.20mm Standard @BBL P2S`: the pointer filament
  is used in layers 1 to 5 only, as a wall loop and an inner loop, with two body lines between
  it and the edge (`renders/knob-two-color-first-layer.png`); six filament changes (one per
  layer up to layer 6); 26 to 28 min and 2.6 to 3.7 g per knob with the prime tower. Slimmer `h5`
  at other layer heights: four layers at 0.24 mm, six at 0.16 mm, eight at 0.12 mm, eleven at
  0.08 mm.
- **In the slicer**: open the 3MF (OrcaSlicer imports the geometry and the filament numbers, not
  a project) with two filaments already in the project. Opened in a project with one filament,
  the `pointer` part lands on filament 1 (seen in the user's OrcaSlicer, 2026-11-12): pick the
  `pointer` part in the object list (Process panel, Objects) and set it to filament 2. Use
  0.20 mm layers with the 0.2 mm first layer.
- Fit: print `knob-<width>-h4-two-color.3mf` (the user's pick on the P2S, 2026-11-12). For
  another printer or material pick the fit with the gauge as below, then print the two-colour
  knob of that fit.

## Print

- The STL is already in print orientation: top face (pointer pocket) on the bed, open end up,
  no supports. 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers or finer. PETG or PLA.
- 4 perimeters or 100 % infill, so the wall behind the teeth is solid.
- In the slicer preview the 20 teeth should show as small bumps on the bore wall from 2 to 8 mm
  above the bed (the bore's closed end is at the bottom of the print). If the slicer smooths
  them away, turn off any hole compensation or small-feature filter.
- **On the user's P2S, skip the gauge and print `h4`** (`<width>/knob-<width>-h4.stl`, or the
  two-colour `h4`): the user's pick, 2026-11-12. The gauge steps below are for another printer,
  filament or settings, or for a width where `h4` turns out different.
- **Fit gauge of the width you want** (`<width>/knob-<width>-fit-gauge.stl`, sizes
  under "Fit gauge"): already in print orientation, top faces on the bed, no supports, with the
  same filament and settings as the knobs. Snap or cut the knobs apart at the thin links and trim
  the link stubs off the top edge. Count the notches in the base rim (the end with the hole).
  1. Try the 5-notch knob first (`h5`, the old `t1`).
  2. Then go both ways from it: 4 .. 1 notches have a smaller hole each (snugger on the shaft,
     teeth wedge earlier, sit higher), 6 .. 8 notches the same hole with shallower teeth (easier
     on, less bite). Push each on lightly, turn it a little until the teeth drop into the knurl,
     then push straight down with a firm thumb, no more. Do not force one on, least of all a
     slimmest one: the lowest numbers may not go on at all.
  3. Take the one that locks best: it cannot be twisted on the shaft (hold the knob and turn
     the pot to an end stop: no click, no play; do not lean on the end stop), does not rock, and
     still goes down to about 1.5 mm above the panel (or as high as is acceptable).
  4. Print four knobs of that number and width: `<width>/knob-<width>-hN.stl`. The gauge
     knob itself can stay in use: its notches face the panel.
- A fit picked on one width is a first guess for another, not a result: the hole is the same but
  the wall behind the teeth is not.
- Between two fits (one goes down with a little play, the next stops higher), take the deeper
  one if its height is acceptable: it sits about 1 mm higher but holds.
- If the best fit is `h1` or `h8`, the ladder should move: shift `FITS` in the script. A caliper
  reading of the shaft, over the knurl and below it, helps to place it.
- Bore and teeth are one pair per fit in `FITS`. If a fit locks against turning but still rocks,
  or holds well but is hard to push over the plain shaft, say which: the bore can be set apart
  from the teeth.
- Push the knob on with the pointer where the pot's position needs it (9 deg steps).
- Fill the pocket with the white paint pen and wipe the top face before it dries (or print the
  two-colour knob, above).
- Puller: the STL lies flat on its side (10 mm tall), no supports, 0.4 mm nozzle so the hooks are
  two perimeters. Layers then run along the legs and hooks; do not stand it upright. 100 % infill
  or 4+ perimeters (the legs are 2.8 mm). Trim any elephant foot off the hooks, it thickens them.
- Other fits, widths or shaft lengths: edit `FITS` / `BORE_D` / `SPLINES` / `TIP_TAPER` / `KNURL_*` / `SIZES` / `SHAFT_*` / `BORE_DEPTH` / `GAUGE_*` / `PULL_*` at the top of `tools/build_knobs.py` and rebuild. A new width in `SIZES` gets its own folder and gauge and needs a `PULL_TIP_R` entry.
